#The Operational Land Use and Transport Microsimulation (OLUTM) Model 
Is a computer-implemented land planning system designed to generate at least one conceptual fit solution to a user-defined land development problem. The model system is operational when executed on a computer, enabling the creation of at least one candidate solution to the land development problem. A candidate solution incorporates a plurality of engineering measurements applicable in the development of an undeveloped land site. Existing GIS data is collected for the selected undeveloped land site. The models interact to maximize the utility behavior of agents (people and vehicles) and objects (jobs, buildings, land uses, etc.) using sociodemographic data and a montecarlo function to simulate random decisions based on the potential value of loss or gain in a two-stage decision-making process. The decisions agents make have physical effects on the urban land use system, enabling the model to quantitatively evaluate the candidate solution based on its cost. The model can be iterated to test problem-solving strategies and scenarios by manipulating the engineering measurements of the candidate solution to achieve a more quantitatively fit solution. The model provides quantitative, geo-located data in the form of tables, maps, figures, and 3D imaging to illustrate the fit solution to the land development problem. 

#Contribution/Novelties
1- A significant probability of residential to mixed land-use change was found for middle-aged farmers with risk-taking lifestyles and upper median incomes venturing home-business entrepreneurships (HBEs). HBEs diversify rural livelihoods with non-farm activities that capitalize on local products and complement rural labor. This is important to sustain the environmental stability of peri-urban areas. 
2- Microsimulation methods have focused on modeling urban areas and have not addressed post-disaster mobility in rural areas.
3- Unlike other models, this model was designed to allocate primary activities and simulate the attraction of people from various scales of a city to evaluate land use growth in a rural development scenario, and help local governments formulate pre-disaster recovery plans.
